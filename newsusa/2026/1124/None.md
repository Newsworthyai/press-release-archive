# China’s Nuclear Energy Advantage

(NewsUSA) - Both the United States and China are channeling nuclear energy to help meet the needs of an industrial future, but China currently maintains a strong advantage for building the first generation of small modular reactors (SMRs), according to a new report from the Special Competitive Studies Project (SCSP), a nonprofit and nonpartisan initiative with a goal of making recommendations to strengthen America's long-term competitiveness in artificial intelligence.

Although the U.S. leads in terms of innovation regarding SMRs, China leads in the other four of five dimensions on the SCSP’s Tech Scorecard. Developed by SCSP’s experts, the Tech Scorecard methodology involves a five-point analysis of elements including Innovation Leadership, Market Ecosystem, Talent Pipeline, Industrial Capacity, and National Leverage.

Innovation Leadership. The volume of developers and designs for SMRs gives the U.S. its advantage in Leadership Innovation. However, China has taken the lead in both volume of nuclear research and collaboration between industry and academia. Notably, Chinese authors published more than three times as many papers as American authors in the scientific journal Nuclear Engineering and Design in 2025.

Industrial Capacity. China’s access to uranium, and greater capacity for manufacturing, qualified nuclear suppliers, and construction labor give it the advantage for now, according to the SCSP experts. However, the United States does retain strengths in some areas including simulation, national laboratories, and private innovation, and the scorecard rates its trajectory for industrial capacity as improving.

Market Ecosystem. China leads in deployment economics, with documented development of five designs for SMRs across four organizations, and with one completed and one expected to come online this year. By contrast, the U.S. is developing 27 designs through at least 22 companies. The concentration of resources on fewer projects helps China build more quickly, with state-owned enterprises running the nuclear buildout. The U.S. model of private demand, greater entrepreneurial scope, and early allied-market commitments can still succeed “if the first projects reach operation quickly enough to unlock follow-on orders,” according to the Tech Scorecard report.

Talent Pipeline. Numbers matter, and China’s large nuclear workforce allows it to retain a strong lead in manufacturing and construction of SMRs now and potentially in the future. However, the U.S. maintains a slight edge in two areas: top-tier nuclear education and the ability to attract international talent.

National Leverage. China’s funding for domestic nuclear programs dwarfs that of the United States, with at least $26 billion spent in 2025, according to SCSP researchers, compared to the $1.69 billion Department of Energy budget in the U.S. China’s coordinated industrial policy and direct control of its largest nuclear producers and consumers add to its advantage, but recent regulatory changes should help the United States become more competitive, and it retains a global lead in exporting both SMRs and conventional nuclear technology.

Overall, although the current comparison favors China, this lead is not guaranteed, according to SCSP. “With the right policies, attention, and funding, the United States can still win,” the experts agreed.
