# What to do after a crash with a suspected impaired driver

(NewsUSA) - A crash can leave you dealing with injuries, missed work and questions about what happened. If you suspect the other driver was impaired, getting help and preserving available evidence can protect your health and help establish the facts.

In 2025, 906 people died in Texas crashes involving a driver under the influence of alcohol, according to the Texas Department of Transportation. A separate TxDOT report shows that 310 of those killed were people other than the drinking driver. The consequences reach passengers, other road users and families.

Get help first

Call 911 if anyone is injured or there is an immediate danger. If you suspect impairment, tell police what you observed and follow emergency instructions. Seek medical attention for injuries. Your safety takes priority over collecting photographs or questioning anyone.

Document what you actually observe

If it is safe, exchange contact and insurance information, photograph the vehicles and surroundings, and get witnesses' contact details. Note the time, location and road conditions. The Texas Department of Insurance recommends gathering these details to help with the claims process.

Attorney Ryan Thompson of Thompson Law Injury Lawyers, known as Lion Law, emphasizes reporting specific observations. Tell police what you personally saw and heard, including statements the other driver made. You do not need to diagnose intoxication or confront the driver.

Preserve evidence beyond the scene

Save any dashcam recording and keep photographs, medical records, bills and insurance correspondence together. Tell your attorney about nearby businesses or vehicles that may have recorded the crash so available footage can be sought before it is overwritten.

If injuries prevented you from gathering evidence at the scene, other evidence may still be available. Witness accounts, police findings and physical evidence can help establish what happened.

Understand the possible routes to recovery

Depending on the facts, an injured person may pursue a claim against the at-fault driver, whose liability insurance may cover damages. Your own uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, if you have it, may also help when the responsible driver has no insurance or insufficient coverage, subject to policy terms and limits.

In Texas, an alcohol provider may also face a dram shop claim. This requires proof that, when alcohol was provided, it was apparent to the provider that the person was obviously intoxicated to the point of presenting a clear danger to themselves and others, and that the intoxication was a proximate cause of the injuries. Serving someone who later crashes does not automatically establish liability.

The available claims and potential recovery depend on the evidence and applicable law. An attorney can explain how those rules apply to an individual incident.
