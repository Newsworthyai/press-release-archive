# BookTrib’s Bites: What We Survive, What We Become

(BookTrib) - “Redemption Initiative: The Good Samaritan” by C.L. Morrissey

Once, Desi Delgado was exactly the kind of criminal society feared: violent, impulsive and consumed by addiction. Eight years in prison changed the course of his life, but not the weight of his past. Now a successful businessman and co-founder of a halfway house, Desi is determined to keep others from repeating his mistakes. When a violent crime strikes close to home, he creates Redemption Initiative, a covert program that uses immersive technology to force offenders to confront the full reality of the harm they've caused.

But can accountability be engineered? Can the process unlock the innate ability to be reformed? Can true remorse be measured and verified? In “Redemption Initiative: The Good Samaritan,” C.L. Morrissey blends crime fiction with provocative moral questions, exploring the uneasy space where punishment ends, redemption begins, and justice itself is reimagined.

Purchase at https://tinyurl.com/redemption-initiative.

“The Realm of Ruin” by Carson Zan Hughes

In “The Realm of Ruin,” exile is not an escape from the world — it is the place where the unwanted are sent to be forgotten. For Nyla, a fiercely loyal hunter torn from her family, and Jester, a bold teenager who refuses to surrender, the Realm is a brutal wasteland of monsters, outlawed magic and secrets no one dares name.

After barely surviving an attack on their village, they are drawn into a monster-hunting guild led by Dice, a leader whose easy smile hides hard-won experience. But each hunt reveals that the Realm is more than a prison. It is a crucible, built to test, break and transform those trapped inside it. Dark, fast-paced and filled with danger, Carson Zan Hughes’ fantasy adventure asks what survival costs — and what someone must become when the world has already cast them aside.

Purchase at https://amzn.to/4eE87O4.

“Casey Dancer: A Memoir About Dating, Stripping, and a Little Hot Yoga” by Kristin Casey

At 39, nearly a decade sober and struggling through a real estate slump, Kristin Casey returns to the work she once thought she had left behind: stripping. To her surprise, stepping back into Austin’s clubs restores not only her finances but also a sense of confidence, purpose and control.

Then she meets Lalo, a married former drug dealer whose charm and volatility pull her into an intense relationship. As romance, financial pressure and old emotional wounds collide, Casey begins confronting the patterns that have shaped her choices — especially her attraction to unavailable men and her fear of depending on anyone.

Interspersed with candid entries from her popular stripper blog, “Casey Dancer” is unfiltered, sexually frank and frequently very funny. Hot yoga becomes both refuge and metaphor as Casey works toward greater strength, firmer boundaries and a life defined on her own terms. It is a memoir about sex work, sobriety and the difficult practice of becoming fully yourself.

Purchase at https://amzn.to/4aG3jXj.

“What the Trees Remember” by Abigail Cutter

At 93, Dora Minor has spent most of her life atop Horseshoe Mountain, carrying secrets nearly as old as the trees surrounding her home. When Hurricane Camille unleashes a catastrophic flood across Nelson County in 1969, Dora faces the storm with only her two dogs — and the memories she has long avoided.

Her story reaches back to post-Civil War Virginia, where she is raised by her fiercely independent grandmother Alma in a Quaker family committed to racial equality. When Dora’s father helps open a school for Black children, the family becomes a target of violent resistance. Loss, betrayal and a horrifying act Dora witnesses as a child shape the isolated woman she becomes.

Moving between Dora’s coming of age and the night of the flood, Abigail Cutter’s deeply researched historical novel explores racial violence, moral courage and the weight of silence, while finding resilience and hard-won hope in the natural world.

Purchase at https://amzn.to/4vPefto.
