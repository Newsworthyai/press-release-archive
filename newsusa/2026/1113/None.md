# Hardwood Flooring: Six Solid Choices for Your Home

(NewsUSA) - When it comes to home flooring, solid American Hardwoods™ have been our gold standard since the nation’s founding—and they still are. Few materials can match the durability of oak, walnut, maple, ash, and other favorite species, which can be sanded and refinished for decades, outlasting kids, pets, foot traffic, and trends—and somehow looking better for it. They’re also remarkably practical, available in multiple widths, grades, and finishes, and easy to install. “American hardwoods are grown, harvested, and milled right here, giving architects and builders a reliable, high-quality supply,” notes Ian Faight of the American Hardwood Information Center. “Nothing rivals hardwood’s warmth and natural variation, and as a low-emission, renewable resource, it supports healthier interiors and everyday well-being.” Here are six examples of what it can do for your home.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/Vermont_Plank_Hard_White_Maple_0.jpg)

In East Greenwich, Rhode Island, this low-slung modern home by Estes Twombly + Titrington Architects blends into its riverside setting. Inside, hard white maple runs underfoot through the kitchen, dining, and living area—an expanse of 5-inch-wide boards from Vermont Plank Flooring, finished with the company’s Killington Peak stain, which draws out the wood’s warm, golden-brown tones. Clear-finished maple window frames, trim, and cabinetry continue the material palette, grounding a light-filled, sustainably minded interior.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/6.%20vermont%20plank%20custom%20ash%20stair%20case_0.jpg)

Circled by a delicate wrought-iron balustrade, a flight of select-grade ash treads winds gracefully up the custom spiral stair in a new shingle-style house by Arnold Design Group in Charlestown, Rhode Island. The light-toned wood from Vermont Plank Flooring gives the traditional architectural element a clean, modern look—one that extends to the landing, which comprises 7-inch-wide boards in the same wood, some laid in a herringbone pattern with walnut accent borders for added visual interest. A clear, low-sheen Craftsbury finish plays up ash’s naturally pale, subtly striped grain throughout.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/ZED%20Wellesley%20hi%20rez_0.jpg)

Architect Stephanie Horowitz of ZeroEnergy Design used old-growth white oak, quartersawn for extra stability and a distinctive ray-fleck grain, for the flooring in a house in Wellesley, Massachusetts, constructed to rigorous green-building standards. The dining room’s solid white-oak table, left with its natural splits and knots intact, echoes the floor’s warm tone underfoot, while wire chairs and vintage milk-bottle prints keep the look from feeling precious. True to the house’s eco-minded design, the flooring is not covered by rugs or carpet, which Horowitz avoids as sources of dust and allergens.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/7.%20vermont%20plank%20walnut%20flooring%20-%20bedroom_1.jpeg)

In a 19th-century Boston townhouse renovated by Stern McCafferty Architecture + Interiors, roughly 4,000 square feet of select-grade walnut from Vermont Plank Flooring runs throughout, including this child’s bedroom, where a built-in daybed nook is wrapped in birch-tree wallpaper. Milled in 6-inch-wide planks and finished on-site with Hartland—a water-based, satin-sheen treatment—the wood comes from black walnut trees, slow-growing natives of the eastern U.S. prized for their fine grain and rich, chocolate-brown heartwood. The flooring honors the home’s history while giving the interior a cleaner, more contemporary feel.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/Hull_260710_DightonMA-050-Edit_0.jpg)

In a whole-house renovation in Dighton, Massachusetts, natural character-grade white oak from Hull Forest Products extends into unexpected territory: the primary bathroom, where 8-inch, live-sawn planks run past the vanity toward a freestanding tub and glass shower. It’s a bold choice, but a sound one—white oak's tight, tyloses-blocked cell structure resists moisture better than many woods, and its density and dimensional stability help it withstand humidity and heavy foot traffic. Even so, area rugs and prompt spill cleanup remain essential. Finished on-site with clear, water-based polyurethane, the reward is underfoot warmth and beauty nothing else can match.

![](https://newsflow.newsusa.com/sites/default/files/inline-images/Vermont_Plank_Red_Oak_White_Wash_3.jpg)

On a Rockport, Massachusetts, peninsula, Ruhl Studio Architects raised an existing house on concrete pilings—both to clear flood requirements and to give the upper level unbeatable ocean vistas. Red oak from Vermont Plank Flooring, laid in 6-inch-wide boards and finished with low-sheen Waltham stain for its warm white overtones, gives the open kitchen, dining, and living space a light, airy foundation. Designed for three generations under one roof, the house pairs the flooring with white-painted poplar walls and steel stairs for a clean, resilient backdrop to the million-dollar view.
