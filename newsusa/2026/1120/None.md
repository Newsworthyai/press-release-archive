# What to Look for When Choosing Child Care, According to New Research

(NewsUSA) - A new national study shows how child care providers are investing in safety, learning and the teachers who care for children every day

Finding child care you can trust is one of the biggest decisions a family makes. It can also be one of the most difficult. Parents want a safe place where their child can learn and grow, and many are also trying to find care that fits their budget and work schedule.

New research from Tyton Partners offers a look at what some of the country's larger child care providers are doing to meet those needs. The study reveals how some of these organizations help close critical gaps in access and quality nationwide and examines the investments these providers are making in early childhood education and the support they provide their teachers.

“Solving America’s child care crisis requires us to use every resource available to support children and working families,” said Radha Mohan, Executive Director of the Early Care Education and Consortium. “Every family deserves access to safe, affordable, high-quality child care that supports their child’s development and gives parents peace of mind, regardless of whether the provider is public, nonprofit or for-profit. Parents should be able to choose who cares for their children based on what works best for their family. This research highlights how major child care providers are setting higher expectations for the entire industry through their meaningful investments in high-quality early learning programs. These providers are creating a path toward a future where families have a variety of quality child care options that fit their unique needs.”

What Parents Can Take Away

The research points to several things leading child care providers offer that families should ask about when comparing programs or touring a child care center.

* What do you do to support teachers? Seventy-nine percent of large, national providers surveyed exceed state requirements for staff training. According to the survey, participating providers pay lead teachers nearly 12% more than the industry average. Many also offer benefits, including health insurance, retirement plans, paid time off and child care tuition discounts. More than 93% of responding providers offer medical insurance, 96% offer retirement plans, and all offer paid time off and child care tuition discounts.
* How will I know how my child is doing? More than 93% of surveyed providers track and share children's daily activities and developmental progress with families.
* Does the center have tuition assistance programs? Many of the large child care providers surveyed participate in a variety of tuition assistance programs. These include state and federal child care tuition support, Head Start, as well as state-funded pre-kindergarten programs. Families should talk with their provider about the tuition assistance programs they may qualify for.
* What quality standards should I look for? Behind every high-quality child care program are dedicated educators, safe learning environments and resources that help children and their teachers grow and develop. Quality is more than a safe building and strong curriculum: It’s also regular communication with families and continued investment in educators, facilities, curriculum and professional development. In addition, more 25% of surveyed providers are nationally accredited, compared to only 5% of center-based programs nationwide.

This research reinforces that no two families have the same child care needs. Child care is essential to the economy as it allows parents not only to work, but to be able to focus on their work knowing their child is safe and well-cared for. Families need to be able to choose the child care option that works for their unique needs. A healthy and robust child care system includes public schools, nationwide community-based providers, nonprofit organizations, and local or home-based programs, as such as system gives families more options to find the right environment for their children.
