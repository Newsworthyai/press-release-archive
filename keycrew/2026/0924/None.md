# Is It a Good Time To Sell a House in Beaverton in 2026?

Homeowners in Beaverton, Oregon, weighing whether to list this year are running into a market that has changed shape over the past few years. According to Carey Hughes Homes, a residential real estate team with 26 years of experience in the Beaverton and Portland metro area, the honest answer depends on both the numbers and the homeowner’s own circumstances.

 “Most importantly, it’s time to sell when it’s the right time for a homeowner on a personal level, depending on what is happening in their life,” says Carey Hughes. “If a homeowner isn’t entirely ready, it’s a very difficult process on so many levels.” Once that personal readiness is in place, the market conditions come into focus, and right now those conditions favor buyers more than they have in over a decade.

 The Market Has Shifted, But Prices Haven’t Collapsed Beaverton is moving toward more of a buyer’s market. Homes are still selling, but buyers are negotiating firmly and taking their time. Hughes is clear that this does not mean prices have dropped sharply. Sellers simply should not expect the results they might have seen in 2021.

 Buyer expectations have been dampened by interest rates and the sheer number of choices now available. For the first time in more than ten years, the local market has reached a genuine balance between supply and demand, giving buyers more room to be selective.

 Why Pricing Precision Matters More Than Ever With more competing inventory, a seller’s position has changed noticeably from a year ago. “You absolutely have to hit price on the nose in order to make the marketing go far and reach the buyers,” Hughes explains. Homes priced outside that sweet spot see fewer showings, fewer website views, and lighter turnout at open houses.

 The consequence is longer time on market and, ultimately, tougher negotiations once an offer does come in. This is not the environment to test a high number and see what happens. Realistic pricing from day one is what keeps a listing competitive.

 What Waiting Another Year Could Cost You Beaverton has been going through a quiet, gradual price adjustment over the past four years, driven largely by interest rates that have stayed close to seven percent and are currently at a 15-month high of slightly over seven percent. Averages have remained fairly flat, but Hughes is seeing prices soften on a month-by-month basis, typically in the range of one to three percent below the prior year.

 Current forecasts do not point to relief on that front next year. For a homeowner who is on the fence, the cost of waiting is not just financial. There is a human cost as well. Hughes points out that people who delay a sale after their life has already moved on, whether for a job change, a growing family, or a life transition, often end up in prolonged stress, sometimes renting out the home indefinitely while waiting for the market to shift in their favor.

 The Personal Timing Question Sellers Can’t Ignore Hughes’s advice comes back to the same starting point: identify whether there is a real, life-driven reason to move. Downsizing for retirement, relocating to be near family, a job change, or a new chapter after a divorce or marriage are the situations that make a move worth pursuing even in a market that requires patience and careful pricing.
