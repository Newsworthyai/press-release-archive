# Near Camelback Mountain, Buyers Are Finding Privacy Without the Paradise Valley Acre

Seasonal and relocating buyers in the Camelback corridor are giving up big lots in exchange for security, low upkeep and a central location.

 The trailhead is at the end of the street. The gate is staffed around the clock. Phoenix Sky Harbor is about eight miles away. In the small communities tucked against Camelback Mountain, that combination is drawing buyers who want a winter home they can close up for months, or a permanent base without an acre of land to look after.

 For decades, privacy near the mountain meant a big lot in Paradise Valley, where homes commonly sit on an acre or more. Many of today’s buyers are weighing that trade-off differently.

 Where Buyers Come From Claire Ackerman, Team Lead of The Ackerman Team at Compass in Scottsdale, says buyers in the Camelback corridor are arriving from says buyers in the Camelback corridor are arriving from California, Washington, Texas, Colorado and the Chicago area. The mix is shifting. “We see a lot more East Coast coming now than just the West Coast,” she says.

 Some move for work, as large employers relocate staff to the Valley. Others are retired, or still working remotely, and buy a second home. Some come to be closer to children and grandchildren who already live here. At this price point, Ackerman says, most are adding a home rather than replacing one; buyers who sell elsewhere are usually moving permanently.

 Taxes and Weather Relocating buyers frequently cite taxes, Ackerman says, pointing to higher property taxes in the states they are leaving. Weather is the other draw. January and February afternoons often reach the 70s and 80s, although desert nights cool quickly and frost sometimes settles on golf courses in the morning.Seasonal residents typically stay from November through April or May. That gives them up to eight months of mild weather before they head home for the summer heat.

 Guard-Gated Scarcity

 For owners who are away for months at a time, security shapes the search. Gated communities are common in Paradise Valley, but Ackerman counts only a handful with a staffed gatehouse. Arcadia is mostly open neighborhoods. Most of the Valley’s guard-gated communities are in North Scottsdale, a significant drive from Camelback.

 Echo Canyon, an 82-home community on the north side of the mountain, is one of the few close in. It has 24-hour manned security at the entrance. “You can’t just pull up and get in the gate,” Ackerman says.

 Phoenix Address, Paradise Valley Setting Echo Canyon homes carry a Phoenix address, yet residents pass the sign welcoming them to Paradise Valley on the way in. The position gives buyers Paradise Valley surroundings without its typical acre lots, along with less land to maintain.

 Lot size brings practical differences too. Ackerman notes that some larger hillside lots in the area rely on septic systems, while the smaller lots in Echo Canyon connect to city sewer.

 Life Near the Trailhead The community sits beside the parking area for the Echo Canyon trailhead, which climbs toward the rock formation known as the Praying Monk. Its streets rise through several elevations, so even a walk around the neighborhood has some grade.

 Shared amenities are being renovated, with completion expected in January 2027. The work includes a redone pool, a new cabana with a small clubhouse and rec room, and refreshed tennis courts.

 Within a couple of miles are the restaurants around the Global Ambassador hotel, including Steak 44 and The Henry, and The Village health club. Scottsdale Fashion Square is nearby, and Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport is about eight miles away. None of it is walkable, Ackerman notes, but all of it is close.

 Lock-and-Leave Design What buyers ask for reflects part-time living. Ackerman says luxury buyers are focused on craftsmanship, natural materials, integrated kitchen appliances, considered lighting, functional floor plans and wellness features such as saunas and cold plunges.

 Low-maintenance layouts, such as courtyard homes, suit owners who are away for half the year. So does smart-home technology that lets an owner check cameras, adjust the lighting and heat the spa from an app before arriving.

 As more buyers split the year between Arizona and their home states, the appeal of the Camelback corridor rests less on land than on access: a staffed gate, a trailhead at the end of the street and an airport a short drive away.
