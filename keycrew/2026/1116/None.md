# Vail, Colorado’s Out-of-Area Buyers Can Now Shop, Finance and Close From Afar

Most luxury homes in Vail, Colorado are bought by people who live somewhere else, and buying one no longer requires much time in town before the keys change hands. Remote previews, specialized lending for foreign nationals and separate, virtual signings have made a Vail purchase something a buyer in Dallas, New York or Mexico can manage largely from home.

 Mark Gordon, broker/owner of Christiania Realty in Vail and a Realtor in the market since 2008, says the shift has changed how buyers first make contact. Where they once walked into a brick-and-mortar office, many now find an agent online and complete much of the search by video before they ever board a plane.

 Where Buyers Come From The Denver metro area remains Vail’s largest source of buyers. Its proximity, roughly an hour and a half away, means Front Range owners use their homes most often, and they own across the range, from one-bedroom condominiums to five-bedroom houses. Beyond Colorado, Dallas and Houston supply a steady share of buyers drawn by summers without heat, humidity or mosquitoes. Miami and South Florida follow a similar pattern, along with the New York metro area, Connecticut and Chicago. California buyers, Gordon notes, tend to stay with California resorts or head to Utah.

 Internationally, Mexico City and other large Mexican metro areas are a significant market. Gordon says many of those families are drawn by safety, a place where children can ride the bus and move around town with an independence that is harder to find in a large city.

 Previewing Homes Remotely 3D tours and floor plans are now standard on new listings, but Gordon says they still fall short of conveying how rooms connect and what a space feels like in person. When a listing catches a buyer’s eye, he walks the home on a live video call over WhatsApp, FaceTime or Zoom. The aim is to answer one question before anyone travels: is this house worth the trip?

 Financing for Foreign Nationals International buyers can finance a Vail purchase through lenders that specialize in foreign nationals. The first step, Gordon says, is explaining how American real estate works, since every country, and every state, handles transactions differently. Communication across borders can add time, but the process remains relatively straightforward.

 Closing Without a Table Vail transactions do not use a shared closing table. Buyers and sellers sign separately, often with online notaries, so no one needs to be in town to close. Some still choose to be. In one recent international closing, buyers from Mexico opted to sign in person in Vail and moved into their new home the same afternoon. “You do not have to come to Vail to sign your documents. However, I would never argue against someone coming to Vail” laughs Gordon.
