# AI Search Left Local Businesses Out of 46% of Answers

Study Finds No Local Business Named in 46% of AI Search Results Across Australia

 Barangaroo, Australia - July 19, 2026 / Snowball Productions /

 A study published by Snowball Productions has found that local businesses were absent from nearly half of all AI-generated answers to everyday Australian consumer questions, raising significant concerns about AI search visibility and how local firms are represented across major generative platforms.

The research, titled The Australian AI Answer Gap, tested 50 everyday questions across four AI platforms: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode. In 23 of those 50 queries, not a single Australian business was named in the response, a result that points to a structural gap between where consumers are asking questions and where local businesses currently have a presence.

Reddit Outranks Local Businesses in AI ResponsesOne of the more striking findings is the identity of the sources AI platforms cited when local businesses were absent. Reddit emerged as the top-cited source across the tested queries, outperforming the websites of local Australian businesses in how frequently its content was surfaced and attributed by AI engines.

The authority gap compounds the visibility problem. According to the data attributed to Snowball Productions, local businesses that did appear in AI-generated answers held roughly a quarter of the authority of the sources ranked above them. That gap in perceived authority, measured through the signals AI systems use to judge source credibility, helps explain why local firms are passed over in favour of aggregator content, forums and international publishers.

The implications extend beyond any single platform. As consumers increasingly turn to AI tools rather than traditional search engines to find local services, product recommendations and business information, the absence of a business from an AI-generated answer is functionally equivalent to not appearing on the first page of a search result, or, in many cases, not appearing at all.

A Generative Engine Optimisation Gap in the Australian MarketThe study frames the findings as a practical lesson in generative engine optimisation, the discipline of structuring and positioning content so that AI platforms are more likely to cite a business when responding to relevant queries. Unlike traditional search engine optimisation, which centres on ranking within a list of blue links, generative engine optimisation requires businesses to think about how AI systems evaluate source authority, content structure and topical relevance when constructing a natural-language answer.

For Australian businesses, the findings suggest that existing digital marketing strategies may not be translating into visibility on the platforms their customers are now using. A business can hold strong local search rankings and still be completely absent from AI-generated answers if its content does not meet the signals that generative platforms prioritise.

The Snowball Productions study draws a direct line between low authority and low AI citation rates, suggesting that businesses investing in authority-building, through structured content, credible backlinks and clear topical expertise, are better positioned to appear when AI platforms construct answers to consumer queries.

Study Methodology and AccessThe Australian AI Answer Gap study was conducted by Snowball Productions as a structured audit of AI platform behaviour across a defined set of queries representing the types of questions Australian consumers ask in everyday contexts. The four platforms assessed, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Mode, were selected because of their growing share of consumer-facing AI search activity in the Australian market.

The findings contribute to a developing body of evidence around AI search in Australia, where the rules governing which businesses get named in an AI answer differ materially from those governing traditional organic search rankings. The study does not name individual client businesses but instead draws on aggregate data to illustrate patterns across industries and query types.
