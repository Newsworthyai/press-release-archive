# Chattanooga Orthodontists Urge Airway Checks for Kids by Age 7

Chattanooga Orthodontic Practice Offers Palatal Expanders and Airway Treatment for Children

 Chattanooga, United States - October 21, 2026 / Scenic City Orthodontics /

 Scenic City Orthodontics, an orthodontic practice in Chattanooga, is encouraging local families to schedule an orthodontic evaluation for their children by age 7, with attention to how jaw and palate development affect breathing as well as how the teeth fit together.

The practice, located at 3326 Jenkins Road, Suite 101, Chattanooga, provides airway orthodontics in Chattanooga TN for children. This approach includes evaluation of airway structure and function alongside conventional assessment of how teeth fit together -- a broader approach than standard orthodontic screening alone.

What Airway Orthodontics Involves

Airway orthodontics considers how the development of the jaw and palate affects the space available for breathing. When the upper jaw is narrow or underdeveloped, the airway can be restricted, contributing to patterns that parents often notice at home. Common signs include mouth breathing and snoring in children, both of which can point to an underlying structural issue rather than a simple habit.

By evaluating airway structure during childhood, orthodontists at Scenic City Orthodontics can identify these concerns while the jaw is still actively growing. Acting during growth periods allows for intervention that can help guide development -- an approach that becomes more limited once skeletal growth is complete.

Palatal Expanders as a Treatment Option

One of the primary tools used in this care is the palatal expander in Chattanooga TN. Scenic City Orthodontics offers two versions: a rapid palatal expander, which is a fixed appliance bonded to the upper teeth and adjusted gradually to widen the upper jaw, and the Invisalign Palatal Expander, a removable option developed within the Invisalign system.

Both appliances work by applying gentle, consistent pressure to the palate to encourage gradual widening. A wider palate creates more space for the teeth and can also increase the volume of the nasal airway. Treatment with a palatal expander can help address airway-related concerns connected to a narrow jaw structure -- though the practice does not represent this or any other treatment as a cure for sleep apnea or any diagnosed medical condition.

The availability of two distinct expander types allows the doctors at Scenic City Orthodontics -- Dr. Tyler Prentice, Dr. Jennifer Ryan, and Dr. Bryan Otis -- to tailor the approach based on a child's specific anatomy, age, and clinical needs.

Early Evaluation Aligned with AAO Guidelines

The practice follows the recommendation issued by the American Association of Orthodontists that children receive an orthodontic evaluation by age 7. At that age, the first permanent molars and incisors have typically come in, giving orthodontists enough information to assess jaw development and identify any concerns that may benefit from early attention.

For parents in Chattanooga and North Georgia, this means the evaluation window is earlier than many expect. A child does not need to have all permanent teeth to benefit from a screening. In fact, waiting until all permanent teeth are present can mean missing the period when palatal expansion is most effective.
