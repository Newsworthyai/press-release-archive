# Calgary Cheque Printer Reports Demand Tied to Recordkeeping Needs

Accounting Teams Drive Cheque Orders as Recordkeeping Needs Persist in 2024

 Calgary, Canada - July 18, 2026 / Print & Cheques Now Inc /

 CALGARY, Alberta, August 2026 - Canadian businesses have widely adopted tap, card, online transfer, and EFT payments, yet cheques continue to represent a significant share of the country's total payment value. According to Payments Canada's 2025 Canadian Payment Methods and Trends Report, cheques accounted for two per cent of total payment volume in 2024 while representing 22 per cent of total payment value.

Print & Cheques Now Inc., a Calgary-based cheque printing company serving more than 46,000 customers across Canada, says those figures reflect what small businesses, bookkeepers, and finance teams continue to encounter in daily operations: cheques are used less frequently, but they remain a consistent tool for higher-value business payments that require a documented paper trail.

"Most businesses are not using cheques for everyday counter sales anymore," said a Print & Cheques Now spokesperson. "They are using them where the payment needs a record, a signature, a remittance stub, or a process that fits the way a vendor, landlord, or contractor already works."

The Payments Canada report found that digital payments represented 86 per cent of total payment volume and 77 per cent of total payment value in 2024. Contactless payments accounted for 58 per cent of transactions. Cheques held 22 per cent of payment value during the same period, trailing EFT at 63 per cent but ahead of credit cards at six per cent.

For Canadian small and mid-sized businesses, that division helps explain why cheque ordering has remained steady. A company may handle most recurring bills through EFT, accept cards from customers, and still maintain printed cheques for vendor payments, rent, payroll adjustments, refund processing, security deposits, and one-off payments that call for additional documentation.

Print & Cheques Now identifies three common factors that sustain business cheque demand:

* Some vendors and landlords continue to request cheque payment for larger or recurring amounts.
* Accounting teams frequently rely on cheque stubs for payment records and reconciliation purposes.
* Certain one-off payments are more straightforward to approve, sign, mail, and file than to process through a digital payment setup.

The company prints Payments Canada Standard 006-compliant cheques and holds CPA Self-Accredited Printer #1010 status. For finance teams sourcing business checks, the decision typically centers on compliance, recordkeeping requirements, and compatibility with existing accounting software rather than any preference for paper-based processes.
