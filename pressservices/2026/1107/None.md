Why Age Seven Matters for Orthodontic MonitoringThe American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic check by age 7, the point at which a child typically has enough permanent teeth in place for a trained orthodontist to evaluate jaw development, spacing, and the relationship between upper and lower teeth. A child does not need to have a visible problem for this evaluation to be worthwhile. In many cases, the assessment simply confirms that growth is progressing normally and no action is needed at that time.

When an early orthodontic evaluation in Bennington, VT does reveal a developing concern, Dr. Efros can determine whether interceptive treatment - such as an expander or other appliance - would be appropriate at that stage. Addressing certain issues during active growth can sometimes mean a shorter, simpler treatment plan later. Early interceptive treatment is not universally necessary, but the evaluation gives families the information they need to make a timely and informed decision.

Treatment Options Across Every Stage of LifeFor patients who do require orthodontic correction, Efros Orthodontics offers a range of treatment approaches. Children and teens undergoing active growth can be treated with metal braces or ceramic braces, both of which attach to the teeth and apply consistent, controlled pressure over time. Clear aligners represent an alternative path suited to many older teens and adults who prefer a less visible option during treatment. Retainers are provided to maintain results after active treatment concludes.

The practice also offers expanders for patients - typically children - whose jaw width needs to be increased to create room for incoming permanent teeth or to correct a crossbite. Each case is assessed individually, and the treatment plan reflects the specific needs of the patient rather than a single standard approach.
