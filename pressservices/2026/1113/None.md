# ALJ Hearings Average 276 Days, Longest SSDI Stage in 2026

Administrative Law Judge Hearings Remain Longest SSDI Stage at 276 Days in 2026

 Phoenix, United States - November 13, 2026 / Pekas Smith: Arizona Disability Attorneys /

 Pekas Smith attorneys explain disability decision waits and when benefits become payable.

PHOENIX, Arizona, 11th Nov. Initial disability decisions averaged 188 days in August 2026, down from 217 days a year earlier, according to national data from the Social Security Administration (SSA). Reconsideration decisions averaged 212 days, down from 239. Hearing-level waits shifted only slightly, from 279 days to 276 days, and remain the longest average among the three stages tracked.

Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) is the federal program that pays monthly benefits to individuals who cannot work due to a qualifying medical condition. Attorneys at Pekas Smith, an Arizona law firm, have outlined how long SSDI takes at each stage of the disability approval timeline. A claim may proceed to Appeals Council review and, in rare cases, federal court.

An SSA decision and the first benefit payment occur at separate points in the process, meaning national decision averages do not indicate when an individual claimant will receive benefits. When a claimant is approved for SSDI, entitlement generally begins in the sixth full month after the date SSA establishes as the disability onset, counting five full calendar months from that date. An exception applies to qualifying claims involving amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). SSDI benefits are paid in the month following the month for which they are due.

Supplemental Security Income (SSI) follows different eligibility and payment rules than SSDI. SSI benefits generally cannot cover a period before the first month after the later of the filing date or the date the claimant satisfies the eligibility requirements.

A state Disability Determination Services (DDS) office reviews the medical evidence and issues the initial decision on behalf of SSA. The office requests records from each treating provider, and a decision may be delayed until those records are received. Following a denial, a claimant may request reconsideration, which is the first stage of the appeals process. A written request must be submitted within 60 days of receiving the notice. SSA generally presumes receipt five days after the notice date unless the claimant demonstrates later receipt, and an extension may be available for good cause.

"A drop from 217 to 188 days is real progress for people waiting on a first decision," said Jeremy D. Pekas, Founding Partner at Pekas Smith. "Of the three stages shown, a hearing is still the longest average wait, and claimants who keep their medical records current are better prepared when that date arrives."

SSA and DDS letters specify response deadlines, and a missed deadline can slow a claim. Claimants can also submit records from every treating provider and monitor claim status through SSA's my Social Security online account.
