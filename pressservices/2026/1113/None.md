# The McFarland Group Publishes Case Study on a $15 Million Sell-Side Transaction

Four Offers in Hand, Closed at $15 Million: A Sell-Side Case Study

 Omaha, United States - November 13, 2026 / The McFarland Group /

 The McFarland Group has published a case study of a sell-side engagement that closed in July 2026, covering a founder-owned electrical testing and field services company sold after twenty years of ownership. The owner came to the firm holding four signed letters of intent, the highest at $8.75 million. The transaction closed at $15 million, with no escrow and a working capital formula unchanged from the one the firm proposed at the outset. The owner continues to lead the business.

The case study is written for owners in a position the firm sees often. Offers arrive before an advisor does, and the owner has to decide whether a process is worth running when buyers are already at the door.

The firm ran a competitive process around the buyers the owner had already been speaking with rather than replacing them. All four remained at the table, joined by three additional qualified parties, with every participant working from identical information on the same deadline. The buyer the owner selected had been in his original group. The terms were not the ones originally on the table.

Because the owner intended to stay on and roll equity, the engagement ran fit questions alongside the economics: growth plans, capital appetite, how much autonomy he would hold after closing, and how each buyer's existing operators described being owned by them. He elected to roll approximately ten percent.

The section of the case study the firm points to first covers diligence. Reaching agreement took about six weeks. Reaching a closing took three more months. Four diligence teams worked through a twenty-year-old, founder-run company, and seven items surfaced that were live risks to the transaction. Several were news to the owner himself, including an employment matter from before the sale process, workforce documentation questions, and three separate threads on the integrity of the company's S corporation election that carried seven-figure back tax exposure if any of them had broken. Each was worked through or cleared before closing.

"We could not promise him a number, and nobody honestly can," said John Bruckner, Managing Partner of The McFarland Group. "What we could promise was a competitive field and the backing to get through the part that comes after the handshake. The months between signature and closing are where deals get retraded, and none of what we found was visible on the day he was close to signing by himself."

The firm has anonymized the company, its owner, and all bidders at the client's request. The full case study is available at themcfarlandgroup.com/case-study/sell-side-electrical-testing.
