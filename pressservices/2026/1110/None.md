# All Water Customs Publishes Guide to Fishing Boat Flooring in Florida

Blood, Chum and Florida Sun: What Actually Ruins a Fishing Deck, and the Flooring That Handles It

 Naples, United States - November 10, 2026 / All Water Customs /

 A fishing deck lives a harder life than the rest of the boat. The upholstery stays clean. The console stays clean. The deck gets blood, chum, sunscreen, spilled coffee, bait juice, braid shavings, and whatever drips out of the bottom of a cooler that has been riding since five in the morning. Then it gets hosed off, parked in the Florida sun, and asked to do it again next Saturday.

That is the real test. All Water Customs, the Florida-based marine flooring shop founded in 2016, has released a practical guide anglers weighing fishing boat flooring options – written for owners who care less about how a deck photographs on day one and more about how it looks after two seasons of redfish, kings, and August heat.

The guide starts from a simple position the shop has held for more than ten years and thousands of installs: on a fishing boat, stain resistance and cleanup speed are not features. They are the whole job.

The Florida Problem: Heat, Sun, and Everything That SpillsFlorida is not a gentle place to own a boat. A deck sitting on a trailer in Fort Myers or on a lift in Tampa Bay can reach surface temperatures that make barefoot fishing unpleasant. Afternoon storms soak it. The sun bakes it dry. That heat cycle repeats hundreds of times a year, and it works on materials in ways a boat show floor never reveals.

Add the fishing itself. Blood sets. Chum smells. Hydraulic fluid from a jack plate finds the low spot. A deck that absorbs anything becomes a permanent record of every good day on the water – and a permanent problem for resale.

Andy Werner, a Naples native who spent 20 years in high-end construction and boating before starting All Water Customs, built the shop around failures he had already watched happen in person. Decks that scorched. Decks that let go at the adhesive. Decks that looked perfect in year one and tired in year two. Every material the company uses today is chosen against those specific outcomes, not against a brochure.

Stain Resistance Starts With the Cell Structure, Not the CleanerMost owners shop for flooring by looking at color and pattern. The more useful question is what happens at the surface when something wet and organic sits on it for six hours in the sun.

All Water Customs fabricates with 100% closed-cell PE foam. Closed-cell means the foam's individual cells are sealed rather than connected, so liquid has nowhere to migrate. Fish blood sits on top. Chum sits on top. Sunscreen sits on top. A hose, a deck brush, and a few seconds of attention take care of all three. The material is 100% stain-resistant, which is a materially different promise than "easy to clean."

That distinction matters most in the places anglers actually make a mess: the bow casting platform, the area around the leaning post, and the strip of deck in front of the fish box where everything gets handled twice.

Traction That Works When the Deck Is Soakedfoamcoat non-skid was designed to be molded, not to be stood on at 30 knots with wet feet and a fish in both hands. Once it wears, it polishes, and a polished deck in a rain squall is a genuine safety issue – especially with kids aboard or an older angler stepping up onto a platform.

Foam decking gives a textured, compliant surface that holds grip wet. It also takes the sting out of a long day standing up. Closed-cell non-slip boat flooring does something else owners notice immediately: it runs cooler underfoot than bare fiberglass, which is the difference between fishing barefoot and hunting for deck boots at the ramp.

For anglers comparing fishing boat flooring in Florida, traction and surface temperature deserve as much weight as pattern selection. Both are things the owner feels every single trip.

Adhesive: The Failure Nobody Shops ForOwners shop for color. Decks fail at the glue line. That gap is where most disappointment lives.

Florida heat cycling puts constant stress on the bond between foam and fiberglass, and non-skid foamcoat makes the bond harder because there is less flat surface to grab. All Water Customs uses a proprietary double adhesive specifically on non-skid floors to address that, and the company's written 5-Year Warranty covers Adhesive and Light Amplification – the two conditions most likely to take a Florida foam deck out of service.

Light amplification is the second one worth understanding. Sunlight concentrated by glass, reflective hardware, or standing water droplets can scorch a foam surface in a localized spot. It is a known Florida failure mode, and it is routinely excluded from the open-ended guarantees sold elsewhere. All Water Customs named it in the coverage instead of writing around it.

The company's stance on this is direct: a warranty that excludes the failures most likely to happen in this climate is a marketing document, not protection. Every client receives the written warranty before installation, not after, so the coverage can be read while the decision is still being made.

Fit Is a Measurement Problem, Not an Art ProblemA fishing deck has hatches, rod lockers, scupper drains, pedestal mounts, and livewell lids. Every one of those is an edge, and every edge is a place where water, slime, and bait debris can collect if the fit is loose.

All Water Customs captures deck geometry with a precision digitizing tool that converts the real boat into a CAD file. The client then receives an electronic Approval Drawing showing full layout, color, and pattern before anything is fabricated – so the pattern around the fish box and the seams at the hatch lines are approved on screen, not discovered at install. Panels are cut in the U.S. to those exact measurements.

The practical outcome for an angler is tight hatch edges and clean drain cutouts, which is what makes a true wash-down possible. Water should leave the boat. So should everything it carries.

Estimates, Cost, and TimelinePricing is where owners usually want straight talk. Here is the shape of it.

* Estimates are free and available same day, with no pre-quote measurement visit required – ten-plus years of boat-specific experience makes an accurate number possible from the information an owner already has.
* Projects generally run from roughly $2,500 to $10,000 and above, with most landing near $3,000.
* Variance is driven by boat size and the amount of design customization requested.
* Typical timeline from measurement to installation is 3 to 3.5 weeks.

All Water Customs sits at fair market rates for the category – below shops charging roughly double on the strength of open-ended guarantees with exclusions buried in the fine print, and above shops saving money on material and install labor. The useful math is total cost of ownership. A deck that holds up and holds its warranty costs less than a deck replaced in two years, whatever the first invoice said.

Who This Work Is For, and Who It Is Not ForAll Water Customs is a good fit for owners who intend to keep the boat, fish it hard, and want the deck handled correctly one time.

It is not the right shop for price-shoppers hunting the lowest bid, and it is not set up for single-piece jobs. That is not a sales posture – it is an honest description of how the shop operates and what it does well. Owners looking for the cheapest number in the market will find it somewhere else, and both parties will be better off.

Named warranty coverage addresses what happens if Florida wins a round anyway.
