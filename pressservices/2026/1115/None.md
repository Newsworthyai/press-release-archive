# Reese Orthodontics Outlines Kids Braces Care in Charleston

What Charleston Parents Should Know About Early Orthodontic Treatment and Kids Braces

 Charleston, United States - November 15, 2026 / Reese Orthodontics /

 Reese Orthodontics, located at 2057 Charlie Hall Blvd STE D in West Ashley, Charleston, SC, has shared guidance for parents on the early orthodontic treatment process for children - from the first examination through routine adjustment visits. The guidance, from Dr. Reese McElveen, addresses a topic many families find unclear: when to start, what to expect, and how kids manage braces in everyday life.

The First Orthodontic Exam by Age 7The American Association of Orthodontists recommends that children receive their first orthodontic evaluation no later than age 7. At this stage, most children have a mix of baby and permanent teeth, which gives an orthodontist enough information to identify developing concerns while there is still time to address them without more complex intervention later.

During the initial visit at Reese Orthodontics, Dr. Reese evaluates the alignment of the jaw, the spacing and position of incoming teeth, and whether the bite is developing as expected. Parents can expect this first appointment to be observational in nature. In many cases, the recommendation following an early evaluation is simply to monitor the child as more permanent teeth come in. Early orthodontic treatment that Charleston families seek is not always a matter of immediately placing braces - it is often about gathering the right information at the right time to plan effectively.

Determining When Braces Make Sense for a ChildNot every child who visits an orthodontist at age 7 will need braces right away. The decision to move forward with treatment depends on several factors, including the severity of crowding or spacing, bite issues such as an overbite or underbite, and whether early intervention would meaningfully simplify future treatment.

When an evaluation shows a Charleston, SC child would benefit from braces, the orthodontist discusses the specific concerns identified and what treatment is designed to address. Traditional metal braces remain a common option for younger patients, as they are effective across a broad range of bite and alignment issues. Dr. Reese and the team at Reese Orthodontics work with families to explain the reasoning behind any treatment recommendation before moving forward.

Daily Care Once Kids Get BracesOnce braces are placed, maintaining them requires consistent daily habits. Food choices become a consideration - hard, sticky, or crunchy foods can damage brackets and wires and are generally avoided during treatment. Children are guided on how to brush carefully around brackets and wires, and flossing with braces in place requires a different technique than flossing without them, often using a floss threader or specialized orthodontic floss.

Parents of younger patients often take an active role in checking that brushing and flossing are thorough, particularly in the early weeks of treatment when new habits are still forming. Families receive guidance on caring for braces at home, and questions about brushing, flossing or food choices can be raised at any visit.

What to Expect at Adjustment VisitsThroughout the course of treatment, patients return to Reese Orthodontics on a regular schedule for adjustment appointments. During these visits, the wires may be tightened or replaced, brackets may be adjusted, and the orthodontist checks progress against the treatment plan. These appointments are typically shorter than the initial evaluation and placement visit, though their frequency depends on the type of treatment and how the case is progressing.

Adjustment visits are also an opportunity to address any concerns that come up between appointments - whether a bracket has come loose, a wire is irritating the gum, or a child is having difficulty with a specific aspect of home care.
