# One Up Outlines Technology Planning Steps for Growing Oregon Businesses

One Up Solutions Northwest Guides Oregon Businesses on Planning Technology Before Hiring or Expansion

 Tigard, United States - September 15, 2026 / One Up Solutions Northwest, Inc. /

 TIGARD, OR 9/14/2026 – Oregon businesses that add employees, open new locations, or replace aging equipment often make each of those decisions separately, according to guidance from One Up Solutions Northwest.

The guidance describes how growing businesses can evaluate their current systems, anticipate technology needs tied to hiring and expansion, and sequence equipment and budget decisions before they become urgent.

New Hires and New Locations Create Overlapping Technology NeedsA single new employee typically needs a computer, software licenses, an email account, security controls, and access to shared systems. A new office adds internet service, network equipment, phones, printers, and additional support requirements on top of that.

Those needs compound when they land on top of aging equipment, a pending software change, or a growing remote workforce. A business hiring several employees over the next year, for example, may be making that decision at the same time an accounting platform needs an upgrade and several computers are approaching the end of their useful life.

One Up recommends reviewing technology needs alongside hiring and expansion plans rather than after the fact, so equipment, licensing, and support can be in place before new employees or locations come online.

An IT Assessment Identifies What Needs Attention FirstAn IT assessment establishes a baseline for a business's current technology before a larger plan is built around it. The assessment is organized around three questions: what needs attention now, what should be planned for next, and what can safely remain in place.

One Up recommends completing an assessment before a major hiring push, an office move, a software change, or an equipment replacement project, and periodically when recurring technical problems suggest current systems are falling behind.

Computer Age Is One Factor in Replacement Planning, Not the Only OneIndustry benchmarks commonly place business laptops on a three to four year replacement cycle and desktops on a four to five year cycle. Computers older than four years are more likely to need repairs and more likely to lose vendor security support.

"Most business owners are really trying to answer a simple question," said Dave Chapman, President of One Up Solutions Northwest. "What’s still working well, and what’s likely to become a problem next. Seeing that ahead of time makes it much easier to plan and budget without feeling rushed."

Chapman advises that age alone doesn’t determine whether a computer should be replaced. He says performance, repair frequency, security update availability, and the ability to run current software all factor into the decision. A computer entering its fifth or sixth year that still runs reliably may not need replacement, while a newer computer that regularly slows down or fails may.

A Budget Timeline Spreads Technology Decisions Over the YearOne Up's guidance organizes technology spending into four categories: issues that need attention immediately, upgrades to plan for within six months, hiring or software needs expected within twelve months, and larger infrastructure projects that can be scheduled further out. That structure helps prevent a single equipment failure or unplanned hire from becoming an emergency purchase.

An IT Roadmap Connects Planning to Business GrowthAn IT roadmap combines assessment results, replacement planning, and budget timing into a single reference. For businesses without an internal technology executive, virtual CIO services provide additional planning support, helping prioritize competing technology projects and connect IT spending to hiring and expansion plans.
