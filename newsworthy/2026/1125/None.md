# Jason Straughan, San Antonio Vistage Chair, Loved 18-Hour Days Until His Heart Stopped at 46

In an episode of The Building Texas Show, I Loved 18-Hour Days Until My Heart Stopped, Jason Straughan, Executive Coach and Vistage Chair in San Antonio, tells host Justin McKenzie that he loved working 16- and 18-hour days until his heart stopped beating at 46 while he was walking around Walt Disney World. Straughan, who leads CEO peer groups for Vistage, explains what that moment taught him about leadership, self-care and culture, and the full conversation, Season 4, Episode 201 of the show, is also on YouTube.

 Straughan says he has "the good fortune of being a Vistage chair." He was a Vistage member for eight years before becoming a chair. Today he brings CEOs from different industries together once a month for a full day under a blanket of confidentiality to help each other solve their biggest business problems, and he meets with each member every month for a two-hour private one-on-one. "The problem when you're sitting in that chief executive role, you have no peers," he says.

 Much of that coaching, he tells McKenzie, is about the place where a leader's personal and professional lives meet. If a child breaks an arm, a CEO walks out of a board meeting. If a company location catches fire during a soccer game, the CEO leaves the game. "The idea of work-life balance just simply does not exist for a CEO or a high level executive," Straughan says.

 He speaks from his own experience. "I was 46 years old working 16, 18 hour days, working eight days a week, and I loved it," Straughan says. He tells McKenzie he believed everyone around him needed him to work that way, until his heart stopped beating at Walt Disney World. What he learned, he says, is that "the reality was I was sacrificing my health for no good reason." "The people that are counting on you are counting on you to take care of yourself just as much as they're counting on you to take care of others," he says. Instead of chasing work-life balance, he now encourages leaders to have what he calls "the discipline to take care of yourself," and that includes sleep. "Humans cannot catch up on sleep," Straughan says.

 The conversation also turns to change. Straughan compares the disruption from AI to the disruption from COVID and says every company in every industry is feeling change. "Right now, a lot of people are making candles in a world where electricity is coming out," he says. His answer is self-education, which he calls one of the key traits shared by the vast majority of the most successful people he knows. Pointing to community colleges, the bookstore and YouTube, he says, "If you're feeling behind right now, start self-educating."

 On culture, Straughan says CEOs usually describe their core values and mission, "the thing written on the wall." He would rather ask frontline employees what they tolerate. "Your culture is what you tolerate," he says.

 That lens shapes how he sees Texas business. Straughan points to a strong labor force, four major metropolitan areas in close proximity to one another, and a favorable view of business and small business across the state. "The culture of business in Central and South Texas is we are a wonderful place to come start a company. We're a wonderful place to build a small to mid-market business," he says.

 Straughan invites viewers to find him at jdstraughan.com or on LinkedIn. "I just like working with people who are out trying to make the world a better place by building businesses and organizations," he says.
