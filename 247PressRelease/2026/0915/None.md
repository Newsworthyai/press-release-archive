# Kognara Releases Public Sector Pilot Framework for Screen-Free, Caregiver-Led Early Learning

WESTBURY, NY, September 15, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- Kognara, a screen-free early learning program designed to help caregivers support childhood development through simple, everyday activities, has released a new white paper outlining a framework for evaluating the program within public-sector and community settings.  Titled A Public Sector Pilot Framework for Screen-Free Caregiver-Led Early Learning, the paper presents a structured approach for public agencies, early childhood systems, home visiting programs, libraries and community partners interested in evaluating Kognara as a practical family engagement resource.   Kognara's Neural Mission Series currently includes 58 caregiver-led activities organized across six developmental stages from birth through age six. The activities are designed around areas including attention, language, memory, problem solving, motor exploration, emotional understanding, early literacy and early numeracy. They require no child-facing app and are designed to fit into ordinary family routines using common household objects or, in many cases, no materials at all.  The newly released framework proposes a 12-month implementation and evaluation pilot designed to determine how effectively Kognara can be delivered through organizations that already have trusted relationships with families. Potential settings include home visiting programs, libraries, early learning centers, pediatric referral networks and family resource centers.   Rather than presenting Kognara as a replacement for existing early childhood programs, screening or professional services, the framework proposes evaluating it as a complementary caregiver resource. The suggested pilot would examine factors including family participation, caregiver use, accessibility, cultural appropriateness, implementation fidelity, safety, equity, cost and measurable changes in caregiver practices or selected child outcomes.  "Kognara was created around a simple idea: some of the most meaningful opportunities for early learning happen through everyday interaction between a child and the person caring for them," said Ann Alexander, founder of Kognara. "This framework is an invitation to take that idea into real communities, evaluate it responsibly and learn what works, for whom and under what conditions."  The white paper emphasizes the importance of independent evaluation. It distinguishes the established research supporting practices such as responsive interaction and guided play from evidence of Kognara's own effectiveness, which would need to be established through prospective study.  Under the proposed model, approximately 300 to 600 families with children from birth through age six could participate across urban, suburban, rural and underserved communities. The framework recommends accessible materials, standardized staff orientation, independent evaluation and data-minimization practices, with broader implementation considered only after reviewing the results.   The framework concludes that Kognara's screen-free, caregiver-led format may offer a practical delivery model for government and community systems seeking accessible ways to support family engagement in early learning. A successful pilot could provide the foundation for a larger independent effectiveness study and future implementation decisions. About Kognara  Kognara is a screen-free early learning program designed to help parents and caregivers turn everyday interaction into meaningful opportunities for learning and development. Through its Neural Mission Series, Kognara provides short, age-banded activities for children from birth through age six, covering areas including language, attention, memory, problem solving, executive function, early literacy, numeracy, social-emotional learning and motor exploration. Designed for use by caregivers with familiar objects and everyday routines, Kognara aims to make research-informed early learning practical, accessible and easy to incorporate into daily family life.
