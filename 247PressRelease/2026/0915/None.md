# Breacher.ai Launches OSES™ Behave, Turning Security Findings Into Bespoke Awareness Training

ORLANDO, FL, September 15, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- Breacher.ai Corp. announced OSES™ Behave, the security awareness training component of its OSES™ (Orchestrated Social Engineering Simulation) platform. OSES™ Behave enables security teams to generate bespoke awareness training using AI, grounded in their organization's own policies, procedures, simulation findings, and relevant threat intelligence.  Traditional security awareness training has often struggled to keep pace with the threats organizations are trying to address. Content is typically developed for a broad audience and released on a fixed schedule, while social engineering tactics continue to evolve across email, voice, video, SMS, QR codes, and other channels.  There is another gap: even training that is current with the threat landscape may not reflect how a specific organization actually operates.  Every organization has different processes for approving payments, verifying callers, resetting credentials, handling sensitive information, and escalating unusual requests. OSES™ Behave is designed to help security teams train against those specific risks rather than relying exclusively on generic awareness content.  "We built this for the awareness team, not around them," said Jason Thatcher, Founder and Chief Executive Officer of Breacher.ai Corp. "Security is not a one size fits all problem. The people who know how a payment actually gets approved at your company, or how a password reset is supposed to be verified, already work there. Our job is to give them the tools, not to hand them someone else's curriculum."  OSES™ Behave connects awareness training directly to the findings generated by OSES™ Simulate. When a simulation identifies a weakness, security teams can create training targeted to the specific behavior, process, or control involved.  The platform supports simulations and training across voice, video, email, Teams, SMS, Deepfake and QR-based social engineering scenarios. Training can be generated from simulation findings, organizational policies and procedures, threat intelligence, news articles, existing content, or created from scratch. Security teams control the content, tailoring, sequencing, and assignment of training to their organization.  Organizations can deliver content through OSES™ Behave or export it to an existing learning management system. Teams can also import their current awareness curriculum into the platform, allowing OSES™ Behave to either complement or replace an established training program.  Simulations involving executive deepfakes, including voice or likeness cloning, require consent from the individual before any cloning is performed. The underlying philosophy is straightforward: security awareness should address the control and policy, process and procedure that failed, and help security teams address threats quickly, not simply teach employees to identify increasingly convincing synthetic media.  "The useful question is not whether someone can tell a deepfake from a real person," said Thatcher. "Fewer and fewer people can do that reliably, and building a training program on that skill is setting up teams for failure. The question is what happens after the call. If the wire still moves, the control fails. Training should fix the control and help the end user learn."  OSES™ Behave is powered by Breacher.ai's AI Concierge, which allows security teams to create simulations and awareness content on demand. A security team can provide a news article, threat intelligence, an organizational policy, or a simulation finding and use the AI Concierge to generate a customized scenario or training module.  OSES™ Behave is available now as part of the OSES™ platform and can also be purchased separately from the advanced simulation engine. Organizations can request a product walkthrough at https://breacher.ai/platform/secure-behavior-management/.  Breacher.ai Corp. builds OSES™ (Orchestrated Social Engineering Simulation), a platform for social engineering simulations, security awareness training, tabletop exercises, and testing of identity verification controls against synthetic media. The company serves enterprise, legal, industrial, and public sector organizations.
