# When Darkness Gets Louder, Littluns Asks Young Readers to Look for the Light

WEST BLOOMFIELD, NY, October 09, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- There comes an age when children begin making more of their own choices.  They choose their friends. They decide whose opinions matter. They encounter books, entertainment, conversations and ideas their parents may never see. And little by little, the world outside the home begins competing with the voices that first taught them right from wrong.  For parents, that transition can be difficult to navigate.  How much guidance is enough? When does guidance become control? And how do you encourage a young person to recognize the difference between an influence that strengthens them and one that slowly pulls them in another direction?  Those questions sit beneath the fantasy world of Littluns: And the Book of Darkness, the award-winning illustrated novel created by the late Mark Glamack, an animator, director, producer and writer whose career included work for Walt Disney Studios and an Emmy nomination for his contribution to Life with Louie. Glamack also served six terms as a governor of the Academy of Television Arts & Sciences.  Although written for readers ages 12 and older, Littluns was designed to operate on more than one level.  Younger readers encounter an adventure about light confronting darkness. Adults may recognize another story underneath it, one about influence, responsibility, temptation, character and the consequences of the choices people make. And then some adults will go even deeper as they read between the lines.  That distinction was intentional.  Glamack believed there was room for a story that could appeal to young adults and grown readers without asking either audience to leave its values at the door. He was particularly concerned about the messages young people encounter through popular culture and what happens when those messages are accepted without much thought or conversation.  Rather than simply criticize what was already available, Glamack wanted to create an alternative.  "For some time now, many parents, and others, have voiced their concerns about popular books that send the wrong messages to impressionable, unsuspecting minds," Glamack explained in an interview about the audience he hoped to reach.  His answer was Littluns.  The story does not suggest that darkness disappears simply because someone chooses what is good. Darkness remains present. It challenges, tempts and attempts to redirect.  The question is whether it is allowed to win.  That gives the novel relevance extending beyond its fantasy setting.  Young people are beginning to navigate a world in which influences arrive constantly and from almost everywhere. Adults, meanwhile, are trying to prepare them for decisions they ultimately cannot make on their behalf.  Glamack captured that responsibility in one of the novel's central observations:  "If you surround yourself with good and righteous people, they can only raise you up. If you surround yourself with the others, they will drag you down into the doldrums of mediocrity, and they will keep you there, but only as long as you permit it."  The final words matter: only as long as you permit it.  Because Littluns is ultimately not a story about helplessness.  It is about choice.  The light in Glamack's story does not prevail because darkness never appears. It prevails despite its presence. The characters must recognize what they are confronting and decide what they will allow to influence them.  That gives parents and younger readers two different entry points into the same story.  A younger reader can experience the adventure, its characters and the struggle between opposing forces. A parent or older reader can see the larger questions underneath:  Who are we listening to?  What are we allowing into our lives?  What happens when we surround ourselves with the wrong influences?  And when we realize we have wandered in the wrong direction, do we still have the ability to choose differently?  For Glamack, the answer was yes.  His own description of Littluns emphasized hope, determination and the human spirit, while the book's broader recognition has included Mom's Choice Awards Gold honors and a Dove Award.  Perhaps that is also why Littluns is difficult to reduce to a simple age category.  It was written for younger readers who are just beginning to "live life," as Mark's sister and publisher, Stephanie Glamack, describes it. But it also speaks to adults who have lived enough of it to understand how consequential seemingly small choices can become.  The two generations may read the same pages differently.  That may be precisely the point.  As families head toward the holiday season and search for something meaningful to place in someone's hands, Littluns offers more than an escape into a fantasy world.  It offers something to think about.  Something to discuss.  And perhaps most importantly, something to carry forward after the book is closed.
