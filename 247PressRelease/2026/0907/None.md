# Asscher Cut Diamond Engagement Rings Grow in Popularity

SAN DIEGO, CA, September 07, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- Asscher cut diamonds are attracting renewed interest among San Diego couples looking for a distinctive center stone for a custom engagement ring, according to Carl Blackburn of C. Blackburn Jewelers in La Jolla.  Square with deeply clipped corners, the Asscher cut has broad step facets that create repeating patterns of light and dark. This "hall of mirrors" effect is more measured than the rapid sparkle of a round brilliant cut.  Blackburn has seen growing demand for Asscher cut diamonds among clients who want a ring with historical character but do not necessarily want an antique design. Because the cut can appear crisp and architectural in one setting and softer and more romantic in another, it gives him a strong foundation for creating a ring around the individual wearer.  "An Asscher cut gives the entire ring a clear sense of structure," Blackburn said. "Its proportions and faceting can guide every other decision, from the side stones and prongs to the metal, gallery and band."  Carl Blackburn begins by studying the center diamond rather than applying a predetermined setting to it. The stone's exact outline, corner proportions, table, depth and pattern of reflections all affect how the surrounding design should be developed.   Since the Asscher's large, open facets also make its interior especially visible, clarity and precise cutting take on particular importance.  From there, Blackburn considers whether the setting should extend the diamond's geometry or balance it with contrast. Step-cut side diamonds can continue the center stone's long, orderly reflections across the ring.   Tapered baguettes or trapezoids can create a gradual transition from the square center to a narrower shank, while brilliant-cut accent diamonds introduce a livelier pattern of sparkle around it.  The choice of metal changes the Asscher's character as well. White gold or platinum can blend visually with a colorless diamond and emphasize the clean geometry historically associated with Art Deco jewelry. Yellow gold establishes a warmer frame around the stone, making its clipped corners more pronounced while moving the design away from the expected cool palette.   Engraving can add softness and texture, while a more restrained band can give the same diamond a contemporary, almost minimalist appearance.  Prongs and the structure beneath the diamond receive the same attention. The setting must protect the Asscher's clipped corners without obscuring the outline that makes the cut recognizable.   An open gallery can keep a substantial center stone from appearing heavy in profile, while carefully placed hidden diamonds or hand-finished metalwork can add detail visible only from the side.  The Asscher cut traces its history to Amsterdam in 1902, when diamond cutter Joseph Asscher developed and patented the original design. Its 58 facets, high crown, small table, deep pavilion and cut corners distinguished it from other diamond shapes of the period.  The cut became especially fashionable during the Art Deco era of the 1920s and 1930s, when its symmetry, stepped forms and strong lines fit naturally with the period's architecture, fashion and jewelry. Its popularity later receded as brilliant-cut diamonds came to dominate the market.  Interest returned near the end of the 20th century as collectors and couples rediscovered vintage jewelry and geometric diamond shapes. In 2001, the Asscher family introduced the patented Royal Asscher Cut, adding 16 facets to the original arrangement for a total of 74 while retaining the distinctive clipped-square form.  More than a century after its creation, the Asscher cut continues to move easily between historical and modern design. Blackburn believes that adaptability helps account for its renewed popularity among couples who want something recognizable but less conventional than the most common engagement-ring styles.  "The cut brings its own history and personality, but it does not dictate what the finished ring has to be," Blackburn said. "That leaves room to create something personal rather than simply reproduce a period style."  To begin a custom engagement ring, call C.
