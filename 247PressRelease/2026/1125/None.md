# From Cookie Drops to Drive-Thrus: Small Business Owners Share What Small Business Saturday Means to Them

MIAMI, FL, November 25, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- As Small Business Saturday kicks off the holiday shopping season on Nov. 28, owners behind some of the country's growing brands share how one day of local support turns into new customers, stronger communities and momentum that lasts well past the holidays.   Last Crumb: A Founder-Led Brand That Started Small   Derek Jaeger and Alana Arnold founded Last Crumb in Los Angeles in 2020, baking out of a kitchen smaller than 1,000 square feet that could turn out about 100 boxes a week. The brand grew from its first limited cookie "drop" into a nationally recognized luxury cookie brand, and it now runs a flagship in Williamsburg, Brooklyn. This holiday season, Last Crumb opened a gifting salon inside its future Upper East Side storefront at 990 Lexington Ave., where guests can build their own six- or 12-cookie boxes through Dec. 31.   "Luxury isn't a gold ribbon. It's knowing someone spent three days on the gift you're giving," said Casey Terrell, CEO of Last Crumb. "Last Crumb lets guests build a box that says exactly what they think of the person receiving it."   Estrella Insurance: A Local Agency Owner Serving the Neighborhood   Sharon Roman and her husband, Albert Rosales, own the Estrella Insurance office in Bradenton, FL, where they built an agency rooted in family and community. Roman, who has had a limb difference since birth, faced barriers in her first career in behavioral analysis before she moved into insurance and opened her own agency. Parents of children with disabilities have told Roman that watching her run a business gives them hope for their own kids.   "When people walk into our office, they don't see what I'm missing," said Roman. "They see my smile, my positive attitude and the way we treat our clients. I don't see myself as any different or any less capable than anyone else."   MOOYAH Burgers, Fries & Shakes: Hometown Franchise, Hometown Customers   After flying B-1 bombers for the U.S. Air Force and spending 25 years in technology, Bill Engel could have retired. Instead, he bought the MOOYAH Burgers, Fries & Shakes in Wylie, TX, with his wife and son in 2024, and the family grew sales 30% year over year in its first year of ownership.   "I wanted to work with my son, and this was a great way for us to team up and take our futures into our own hands," said Engel.   Hi Auto: Helping Small Restaurant Operators Handle the Holiday Rush   Local franchisees run many of the restaurants that see the most traffic during the holidays. Hi Auto's AI Order Taker now handles drive-thru orders in English and Spanish at nearly 1,000 locations and serves roughly 200 franchisees, helping owner-operators keep lines moving and orders accurate when staffing runs tight. Checkers & Rally's recently extended its partnership with Hi Auto through 2030 after average tickets rose and the share of orders that needed crew help fell by half.
