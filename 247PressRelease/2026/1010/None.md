# Kevin Trudeau names the belief that stalls most success stories before they start

SCHAUMBURG, IL, October 10, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- The pattern he keeps seeing  Kevin Trudeau built Mega Memory into a course that sold over $450 million between 1989 and 1993. He later wrote the number one best-selling book "Natural Cures 'They' Don't Want You To Know About," which outsold Harry Potter the year it came out. He has since written five more New York Times best-selling books and founded the Global Information Network, a success education university with members in over 150 countries.  Across all of that, Trudeau says he has watched the same handful of beliefs show up again and again in people who never get past a certain point. Not lack of talent. Not lack of effort. A handful of specific, wrong ideas about how success actually works.  He is releasing them here as a myth list, not a motivational list. Each one is paired with what he tells students instead.  Myth: success is mostly about working harder  Trudeau does not teach effort as the variable that matters most. He teaches priority. A person can work sixteen hours a day and still lose ground if the sixteen hours go toward the wrong task.  "Activity is not accomplishment," he says. "You can be busy all day and finish it further from your goal than when you started, because you spent the day on things that felt urgent instead of things that actually moved the mission."  Myth: a dream and a goal are the same thing  People often use "dream" and "goal" interchangeably, and Trudeau says that is a mistake that costs them clarity. In his system, a dream is the big idea, the one that might take a lifetime and might not fully arrive in this one. A chief aim sits under that: something in the 6 to 12 month range that is genuinely likely to happen. Below that is the objective, then the goal, then the task, the single action a person can take today.  "If you cannot name the task in front of you right now that connects to the goal above it, you do not have a plan," he says. "You have a wish."  Myth: confidence comes before results  Trudeau says most people wait to feel confident before they act, and that the order runs the other way for anyone who has actually built something. Confidence is a byproduct of doing the thing, not a precondition for starting it.  "Self-doubt is not evidence that you are unqualified," he says. "It is just a feeling. Take the action anyway, and when you have a success, that builds confidence."  Myth: the people around you don't affect your results  Trudeau says this is one of the more expensive misconceptions, because it is invisible. People rarely notice the ceiling their environment puts on them until they change the environment. He teaches that the standards, habits, and expectations of the people closest to you set a kind of gravity around your own thinking, whether you intend it or not.  "You do not rise above the room you spend the most time in," he says. "You rise to it, or you sink to it. There is very little staying neutral."  Myth: setbacks mean the plan failed  Trudeau's own path included a period where regulators restricted the kind of advertising he could run for his products. Rather than treat that as the end of a direction, he shifted into writing books, a decision that led directly to "Natural Cures" and the six best-selling titles that followed.  "A closed door tells you one path is closed," he says. "It does not tell you the mission is closed. Those are two different facts, and people confuse them constantly."  What he wants readers to do with the list  Trudeau is not asking readers to accept these points on faith. He is asking them to check their own thinking against each one this week: what task is in front of them today, whether it connects to a real goal, and whether the people around them are pulling their standards up or down.  "None of this is complicated," he says. "It is just not what most people were taught. I did not invent these principles. I was taught them, I used them, and now I teach them the same way they were given to me."  Kevin Trudeau is an author and entrepreneur based in Schaumburg, Illinois. He founded the American Memory Institute and built the Mega Memory course into one of the best-selling self-improvement programs of its era. He wrote the number one best-selling book "Natural Cures 'They' Don't Want You To Know About" and five additional New York Times best-selling titles, including "Your Wish Is Your Command: How to Manifest Your Desires." He also founded NaturalCures.com, The World's Best Nutritionals and Organics, and the Global Information Network, a success university with members in over 150 countries.
