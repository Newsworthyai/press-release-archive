# Educational Nonprofit Open Mindset Project Helps Students Build Cross-Cultural Understanding and Human Skills in the Age of AI

AMESBURY, MA, November 17, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- The Open Mindset Project enters its second year with a distinctive approach to preparing college students and recent graduates for an increasingly interconnected workplace: combining a structured curriculum focused on understanding different perspectives with one-on-one mentoring from experienced professionals. Founded in fall 2025, the nonprofit gives students opportunities to connect what they learn with real-world professional experiences.  The Project's focus on human skills takes on added importance as artificial intelligence changes how people work. Technology can automate or accelerate many tasks, but people still need to communicate across differences, understand other perspectives, adapt to unfamiliar situations, build relationships and exercise judgment. Often called "soft skills," these capabilities help people navigate situations that require collaboration, context and human connection.  Founded by author, entrepreneur and global leadership expert Stacie Nevadomski Berdan, the Open Mindset Project combines four online learning modules with individual conversations between students and mentors known as Global Gurus. The program explores globalization, culture, communication, perspective and career development, giving students opportunities to ask questions, challenge assumptions and consider how these concepts apply to their own goals.  "Students don't need another program that simply tells them what skills matter. They need opportunities to practice those skills, ask questions, challenge their assumptions and connect what they learn with the real world," said Berdan, Board Chair and Chief Executive Officer of the Open Mindset Project. "That combination of structured learning and meaningful mentorship sits at the heart of the Open Mindset Project."  The program's mentors, known as Global Gurus, bring an extraordinary range of professional experience and perspectives to their work with students. They include artists, physicians, technology entrepreneurs, military leaders, cybersecurity experts, human resources leaders and professionals from many other fields. They bring lessons from successes and setbacks and a strong desire to share what they have learned. The Open Mindset Project creates opportunities for students and mentors to connect through genuine human conversation and exchange.  The Project uses the term human skills to emphasize capabilities people develop through interaction and experience, including communication, adaptability, perspective-taking, collaboration and relationship-building. As AI handles or accelerates more tasks, these capabilities help people apply knowledge, work with others and respond thoughtfully when situations require context and judgment.  Early experience points to practical application  Since launching a year ago, approximately 140 students and recent graduates have completed the program. The current fall cohort includes 50 students, while the network of volunteer Global Gurus has grown to more than 175. To date, the Project has worked with 21 colleges, universities and professional organizations.  Results from the Spring 2026 cohort survey suggest that students moved beyond learning about global concepts and began considering how to apply those concepts. By the final module, 100% of responding students said the program helped them think about applying global awareness and human skills to their academic or career goals.  "I would strongly recommend this program. It pushed me to think more critically about how I engage with people from different backgrounds and challenged assumptions I didn't realize I had," said a Spring 2026 student. "It changed how I approach problems, and I think any student who wants to build career skills that translate across cultures and industries would benefit from that same shift."  Mentorship added a practical dimension to that learning. In the final module, 87.5% of respondents gave the highest possible rating when asked whether their mentor connected course content with real-world professional experiences. Among responding Spring 2026 mentors, 92% said their mentee showed real progress in developing a global mindset during the program.  "Mentoring gives you a chance to share what you have learned, but the relationship goes both ways. The students bring their own questions to the conversation, challenging existing narratives and providing fresh perspectives that shape future thinking," said Jason Elliott, Global Marketing & Communications, Nokia, an Open Mindset Project mentor. "That makes the experience meaningful and gives you an opportunity to see your professional experience through a different lens."  Another Spring 2026 student described the value of the mentor relationship. "For me, having scheduled coffee chats with mentors really helps build confidence in having professional conversations and networking effectively. Being exposed to real mentors, hearing about their experiences and developing meaningful relationships with them is such a valuable part of the experience."  Looking ahead, the Open Mindset Project continues to expand its relationships with colleges, universities and professional organizations while growing its community of students, recent graduates, early-career professionals and mentors. The goal remains consistent: to give more people access to the perspective, human skills and professional relationships they need to navigate an increasingly interconnected world. As AI and other technologies evolve, these human capabilities will remain essential to effective communication, collaboration and connection.  About the Open Mindset Project  The Open Mindset Project is an educational nonprofit organization established in 2025 that prepares college students and recent graduates for careers that cross cultures and industries. By the end of 2026, approximately 250 participants from more than 21 universities, colleges and professional organizations will have completed the program. More than 175 accomplished volunteer mentors make up the network of Global Gurus.
