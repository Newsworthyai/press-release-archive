# Pastors From Five Regions Unite in Support of Detained Chairman Lee Man-hee

NEW YORK, NY, September 17, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- On September 10, pastors and faith leaders came together for a two-part international event centered on a shared responsibility of faith: protecting religious freedom and human dignity, while building greater unity among churches through dialogue.   Part 1 of "Human Rights, Religious Freedom and the Essence of Faith" was broadcast internationally from New York, connecting faith leaders across the United States, Brazil, India, Sri Lanka, and Liberia. Part 2, the "1st Word Exchange — Assembly of New York Pastors," continued with an in-person gathering in New York, where pastors from different denominations came together to listen, share, and build relationships through their shared scripture, the Bible.  A Call for Human Rights and Religious Freedom The first part of the event delivered an important message: religious freedom must be protected not only when our own beliefs are affected, but also when people of different faiths face prejudice, discrimination, or injustice.  Pastors from five regions addressed concerns surrounding religious freedom and the detention of 95-year-old Shincheonji Church of Jesus Chairman Lee Man-hee. Chairman Lee, despite his advanced age and health concerns, has been detained in a detention center in South Korea for about 70 days.  "Would the real church please stand up—for justice, human rights and religious freedom. Enough is enough," said Pastor Marc Thompson of Unity Baptist Church of Mattituck, New York, appealing directly to the conscience of the church. "Let Chairman Lee go."  From Chicago, Pastor Lucilla Richards Seaton of Power, Prayer and Praise Ministries warned that the issue extends far beyond one religious community, expressing concern that religious rights are being threatened around the world.  Other pastors based in New York also spoke out about the importance of unity.  Rev. Hallie Foard of Allen AME Cathedral reflected on the strength that can emerge when believers across nations stand together. Despite differences in background, she emphasized that the love for God can unite people and that international cooperation is especially important when individuals face opposition because of their faith.  Bishop John Montalvo of ELIM House of Worship similarly described the gathering as impactful because people of different nationalities, languages, and backgrounds were able to come together in fellowship. In confronting religious prejudice, he emphasized love, respect, understanding, and dialogue rather than attacking those whose beliefs differ from our own.  The pastors' reflections raise a fundamental question: If religious freedom is guaranteed only for those with whom we agree, is it truly religious freedom?  The gathering called on churches to recognize that protecting human dignity and religious liberty is not the responsibility of one denomination, one nation, or one religious community. It is a responsibility that requires all people of faith to stand together.  Building a Beautiful Bridge Through the Word The second half of the event focused on turning advocacy into action.  Faith leaders from different denominations and churches based in New York gathered under the vision of the Beautiful Bridge Project to share their beliefs, listen to one another, fellowship, and seek greater understanding of the Bible.  The purpose of this portion of the event was not to pretend that doctrinal differences do not exist. Rather, it was to create a space where those differences would not prevent pastors from sitting together, listening sincerely, and allowing the Word of God to become a bridge.  Apostle Ralph Pritchard expressed that believers must move beyond traditions and divisions that hinder the work of God. He emphasized listening rather than condemning and appreciated the Word Exchange as a place where pastors could openly discuss scripture and fellowship without judgment.  Pastor Tyrone Freeman of Hope International Church expressed gratitude for the unity he experienced among the churches and expressed his desire to participate in the next Word Exchange.
