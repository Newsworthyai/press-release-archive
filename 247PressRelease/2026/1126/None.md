# Anthony Nayagan's Transformative Innovation Shows Leaders How to Build What's Next Without Disrupting What Works

JACKSONVILLE, FL, November 26, 2026 /24-7PressRelease/ -- As artificial intelligence transforms the way with think and work, Anthony Nayagan's new book guides leaders in their search for clarity, transformation and innovation while maintaining what already works.  "We are overwhelmed by information, yet starved for clarity," Nayagan says. "We double down on analysis -- yet find ourselves drifting further from true transformation."  Corporate leaders are wrestling with integrating artificial intelligence into the workplace, much as society transformed from the industrial age to the information age. Switching from the flip phone to the smart phone resulted in entire markets built around apps. In his latest management book, Transformative Innovation, Nayagan asks how leaders can develop ideas with that kind of potential while keeping their organizations running.  "Transformative innovation is the creation of a fundamentally new product, service, or idea that changes how people live, work, or solve problems," says Nayagan, an executive coach who bridges the world's great wisdom traditions to awaken divine intelligence in leaders, creators and seeks.   The book's distinctive contribution is a framework for nurturing transformative ideas, such as artificial intelligence, into models ready for deployment while existing products, services, policies, and systems continue to operate without interruption. It shows leaders how to give early ideas a dedicated place to develop, test them under real conditions, and prepare them for adoption once they are ready.  Nayagan shows how companies and universities can put this framework to work. Students and faculty develop and test ideas with industry mentors, while operating teams continue to serve customers and meet their commitments. When an idea is ready for adoption, the organization has something it can put to work.  For the serial entrepreneur, thought leader, policymaker, or restless inventor in the family, Nayagan asks the question: "What are we still trying to improve that someone else is about to reinvent?"
