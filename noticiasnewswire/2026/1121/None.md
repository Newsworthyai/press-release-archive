# New Survey Reveals Financial Anxiety, Hidden Debt, and Midlife Pressures Behind the $1 Trillion Holiday Shopping Season

FORT LAUDERDALE, Fla., Nov. 20, 2026 /Noticias Newswire/ — As holiday spending in the United States is projected to surpass $1 trillion, with the average household expected to spend approximately $1,654, millions of Americans are entering the busiest shopping season of the year under mounting financial pressure.

 Behind the excitement of holiday deals, gift-giving, and family celebrations lies a less festive reality: growing credit card balances, financial uncertainty, and the emotional toll of managing debt.

 According to Consolidated Credit’s Credit Card Anxiety Index, 61% of Americans are concerned about how much they owe on their credit cards. Among those experiencing credit card-related anxiety, nearly half say their financial worries negatively affect their mood, while 41% report feeling more anxious about their credit card debt than they did a year ago.

 The findings reveal a troubling contrast between record-breaking holiday spending projections and the financial challenges facing American households. For many consumers, the pressure to purchase gifts, participate in holiday traditions, and keep up with seasonal expenses could lead to greater reliance on credit cards and financial stress that extends well beyond the holidays.

 In response, Consolidated Credit has introduced a free, interactive Holiday Spending Planner designed to help consumers establish realistic budgets, organize seasonal expenses, and better understand the potential cost of financing holiday purchases with credit cards.

 The goal is simple: empower families to enjoy the holiday season while making informed financial decisions that protect their long-term financial well-being.

 Key Findings from the Credit Card Anxiety Index Consolidated Credit’s survey of 1,313 U.S. adults, paired with internal consumer data, highlight several critical trends shaping consumer behavior this holiday season:

 * Midlife Shoppers Face the Highest Stress: Worry peaks among 30- to 44-year-olds (75%) and 45- to 60-year-olds (69%). Millennials and Gen X, who are caught between gifting expectations, housing costs, and inflation account for 71% of Consolidated Credit debt management clients.
* Compounding Interest Is the Top Fear: Among worried cardholders, 34% cite interest piling up as their primary concern, followed by missing a payment (24%) and never paying off their balance (22%).
* Financial Avoidance: More than a third (36%) of worried respondents admit they have avoided checking their balances or statements—nearly double the 20% who have looked into debt advice. Nearly a quarter (24%) say they downright dread or avoid opening their monthly statements altogether.
* Suffering in Silence: One-third (33%) of worried Americans haven’t told anyone how they feel about their credit card debt—including half of respondents in relationships who haven’t confided in a spouse or partner.

 Credit Counselors Share Tips to Avoid Holiday Debt Before Black Friday * Calculate a Holiday Ceiling: Map out cash flow to separate essential bills from holiday funds.
* Don’t forget the extras: Factor in those little holiday expenses—like party hosting, travel, wrapping paper, and experiences—not just the presents under the tree.
* Prevent Statement Shock: Track spending in real time to avoid the holiday debt hangover in January.
* Set Financial Guardrails: Use the free Holiday Spending Planner as your personal coach, helping you with gift, travel, and party planning in minutes. Walk into Black Friday knowing exactly what you can afford.
