# With The Fed Expected By Many To Raise Rates, Actively Managed Infrastructure Capital Bond Income ETF (BNDS) May Be Worth A Closer Look

By Meg Flippin, Benzinga

 DETROIT, MICHIGAN - November 12, 2026 (NEWMEDIAWIRE) - The Federal Reserve left interest rates unchanged during its October meeting, but that doesn’t mean everyone was in agreement. There was dissent among officials, with two Fed officials preferring a quarter-point raise.

 ![](http://newmediawire.s3.amazonaws.com/6af49fccda846f52982a9f3b_1)

 In one camp is the majority of Fed officials who argued it was necessary to hold rates steady to see the cumulative impact of past hikes before acting again. On the other side are the hawkish dissenters, who contend raising rates is necessary to fight persistent inflation.

 Dissent Goes Beyond Fed Meeting

 The dissent doesn’t end with Fed officials, however. The pause in rate actions, which left the Fed’s benchmark federal funds rate in a target range of 3.75% to 4.00%, is expected to be temporary. With inflation rising, many economists and investors expect the Fed to continue to raise rates in the coming months. But by how much and when is up for debate. Investors and economists expect a quarter-point increase in the December meeting. As for what happens in 2027, some are forecasting increases while others are projecting rate cuts.

 Given the current trajectory of monetary policy winds, it's quite possible that financial services provider Infrastructure Capital could be standing on fertile ground. In particular, its income-focused fund called Infrastructure Capital Bond Income ETF (NYSE: BNDS) may see greater interest among investors seeking more robust and reliable total returns.

 Looking further, all of that uncertainty creates volatility in the markets and headaches for income-seeking investors invested in passive mutual funds and ETFs. If the Fed’s actions prompt the markets to move one way or another, these investors aren’t able to react quickly. It's the reason actively managed ETFs are having a moment. Actively managed ETFs are attractive to many investors because they allow managers to adjust duration, credit quality and sector exposure in real time without being locked into a passive index. That is especially true for income-seeking investors. With so much uncertainty about what the Fed will do with interest rates, having a fund manager that can take advantage when opportunities arise can protect capital during downturns and capture higher yields when the rate environment changes.

 Active Income With The Infrastructure Capital Bond Income ETF

 Investors get that flexibility with the Infrastructure Capital Bond Income ETF, which is an actively managed ETF whose primary objective is to maximize income, with a secondary objective to extract capital appreciation. BNDS aims to achieve this by investing at least 80% of its total assets in fixed-income securities, mostly corporate bonds. The fund is geared toward sectors and issuers that feature strong cash flows and pricing power.

 When deciding which fixed-income securities to include in the fund, the management team uses a flexible mix of quantitative and qualitative analysis to evaluate relative value opportunities across fixed-income markets. BNDS then applies a layer of fundamental analysis to the issuers themselves, reviewing things like enterprise value, capital ratios and operating metrics to determine the company's financial health and ability to service debt.

 Hands-On Approach From A Veteran Fund Manager

 BNDS is run by Jay D. Hatfield, who is the founder, CEO and portfolio manager of Infrastructure Capital Advisors. Hatfield brings nearly three decades of experience across investment banking, hedge fund management and portfolio construction to BNDS. Before launching Infrastructure Capital, he co-founded NGL Energy Partners and managed income-oriented portfolios at SAC Capital (now Point72) and Zimmer Lucas Partners.

 That deep background matters. Infrastructure Capital reports that Hatfield's career has been defined by identifying undervalued credit opportunities and structuring strategies to extract reliable cash flows. For BNDS, this translates into a disciplined approach to corporate bond selection, combined with tactical enhancements like option writing.

 With BNDS, it is structured specifically to find and extract asymmetric income-generating opportunities, and it opportunistically employs an option-writing strategy to enhance income.

 Investors may expect the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates in December, but after that, it's anyone's guess what will happen in the coming months. That creates volatility and thus a potential opportunity for income-seeking investors. While you may not be able to get quick action through a passive ETF that can’t capitalize on movements prompted by Fed action, you can with the Infrastructure Capital Bond Income ETF.
