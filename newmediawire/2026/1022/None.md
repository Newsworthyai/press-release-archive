# Fitzroy Minerals and Fleet Space Technologies Use Machine Learning to Define New Untested Targets at the Buen Retiro Copper Project, Chile

VANCOUVER, BRITISH COLUMBIA - October 22, 2026 (NEWMEDIAWIRE) - Fitzroy Minerals Inc. (TSXV: FTZ, OTCQX: FTZFF, FSE: C3Y) (“Fitzroy” or the “Company”) is pleased to announce the results of an integrated seismic and machine-learning (“ML”) targeting study, completed with Fleet Space Technologies (“Fleet”), at the Buen Retiro Copper Project, Copiapo, Chile (“Buen Retiro” or the “Project”). ML targeting generated a ranked set of priority domains (Figure 1) by combining 3D Ambient Noise Tomography (“ANT”) data with prior geophysical surveys, geological models, and approximately 24,400 copper assays from 140 drill holes. The model identified nine areas where the sub-surface characteristics closely resemble the signature of the rocks hosting Buen Retiro’s known copper mineralization.

 Fleet’s regional and infill ANT surveys mapped the Project’s deeper geology well beyond the depths of current drilling. The technique uses natural seismic noise to build a 3D picture of the rock beneath the surface. This gives Fitzroy a 3D framework to trace known mineralized trends and identify new, previously untested opportunities down to a depth of approximately 400 metres, including toward the 100%-owned Sierra Fritis properties to the east. Extensive alluvial gravel cover in the area makes surface geological mapping and therefore target-generation, much more difficult.

 Fitzroy has started a 5,000-metre drilling program using diamond and reverse circulation (“RC”) drill rigs. As drill logs and assay results come in, Fitzroy and Fleet will use the new data to update the ML model. In this way, each new drill hole serves three purposes: testing a specific target, improving or validating the ML model, and helping to inform the next drilling decision.

 Highlights:

 * A ranked portfolio of nine target areas, all completely new and untested, and within 400 m of surface.
* The targets combine Fleet’s 3D ANT subsurface model with Fitzroy’s own geophysical surveys (IP chargeability, IP resistivity and magnetic susceptibility) and approximately 24,400 copper assays from 140 drill holes.
* The targets focus on gravel-covered geology and exclude previously mined ground or areas within 200 metres of an existing drill hole.

To date, the oxide copper mineralisation and its potential to support a low-capital-intensity, near-term production operation have been the focus.

 "Now, however, we have a tool with which to test the exploration optionality of this large IOCG system in one of Chile’s most prolific copper-producing regions. By combining advanced 3D subsurface imaging with machine learning and our extensive drilling database, we can now identify and prioritise blind exploration targets beneath the gravel cover. If the ML targeting is successful it will be a cost-effective discovery tool, creating value for shareholders.”

 From 3D Subsurface Imaging to Ranked Drill Targets

 Fleet’s work at Buen Retiro began with a regional ANT survey commissioned by Fitzroy in early 2026. The ANT survey was designed to build a better picture of the Project’s deeper geology. This identified structures and zones associated with alteration and brecciation at depth, and gave Fitzroy a 3D framework for further exploration.

 A higher-resolution, closer-spaced ANT survey was then completed over the main area of interest. This infill survey improved the understanding of the faults and fractures that are interpreted to be the main control on both permeability corridors and associated mineralization. The infill survey also allowed closer correlation of the ANT data with existing geophysical and drilling data.

 The ML targeting phase then converted this geological and geophysical picture into a set of exploration opportunities. The targets are designed to complement, not replace Fitzroy’s own geological interpretation and drill planning.

 Targeting Method

 The targeting method follows four steps, from copper prediction through to ranked, conceptual targets:

 * Prediction. The ML model learned the relationship between geophysical data and copper assays from existing drill holes in order to estimate a quantified uncertainty for any point in space within the survey area. The aim was to predict zones where copper mineralization with grades above a 0.5% Cu threshold within 25-metre blocks of rock across the study area might be located, together with an uncertainty value for each block. The 0.5% Cu threshold is used to visually identify blocks at, or above, the target grade and does not represent a uniform estimate of copper grade per block.
* Refinement. Blocks were removed from consideration if they fell within the mined-out open pit, mapped gravel cover, or within 200 metres of an existing drill hole, so that target selection would focus on ground with genuine exploration value.
* Domains. The remaining blocks were grouped into nine geographic areas (“domains”), each representing a cluster of blocks with a similar predicted response (see Figure 1) within high-confidence range and interpreted to be geologically significant.

 Drill Planning

 Fitzroy and Fleet are now reviewing drill orientations and sequencing for the list of exploration targets. All of the targets are within the top 400 metres of the surface and Fitzroy has allocated 5,000 metres of combined RC and diamond drilling to test the new areas. As drill logs and assay results come in, Fitzroy and Fleet will compare them against the original target list andupdate the model accordingly. The new results will then feed into future versions of the model, sharpening its predictions and helping guide the next round of drill planning. In this way, each new hole serves two purposes: testing a specific target, updating or validating the ML model, and helping to inform the next drilling decision.

 Technical Context

 The new exploration domains are conceptual exploration targets that have not yet been tested by drilling. They mark areas where the combined geophysical and assay data look similar to Buen Retiro’s known zones of copper mineralization.

 The targets do not confirm the presence, grade, size or continuity of mineralization. The reliability of the model depends on the quality and coverage of the underlying data, and tends to decrease with distance from existing drill holes and with depth. For this reason, the targets are intended to be used alongside conventional geological interpretation and other exploration information, not in place of it.

 About Fleet Space Technologies

 Fleet Space Technologies is an Australian frontier technology company that uses seismic-led, 3D subsurface imaging to help geoscience teams understand what lies beneath the surface. Fleet combines its imaging techniques with a client’s existing geology, geophysics and drilling data, using machine learning to turn that combined picture into ranked recommendations for discovery, expansion and development decisions. As new drilling and other data become available, Fleet’s models can be updated, so new results continue to sharpen future subsurface decisions and deliver drilling predictions that stand up against ground truth.
